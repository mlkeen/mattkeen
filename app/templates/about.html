FHWA Research and Technology Evaluation: Truck Platooning

Foreman, Christina; Keen, Matthew; Petrella, Margaret; Plotnick, Sarah; (Contributor: Moulden, John V.) · FHWA-HRT-20-071 · June 2021

Summary: Evaluates the Federal Highway Administration's investments in truck-platooning research, particularly projects involving partial automation and driver-assistive technologies. The study examines research outcomes, stakeholder awareness, technology development, and the potential for improved fuel efficiency and freight transportation performance.

Findings: FHWA-supported research addressed important technical knowledge gaps, improved industry understanding, and helped establish the feasibility of two- and three-truck platoons for reducing fuel consumption. Stakeholders generally viewed the research favorably, although the evaluation could not isolate the effects of individual FHWA activities or establish the benefits of widespread deployment.

FHWA Research and Technology Evaluation: High Friction Surface Treatments

Coppinger, Kaitlin; Keen, Matthew; Gissel, Jennifer; Rainville, Lydia; (Contributor: Moulden, John V.) · FHWA-HRT-21-014 · August 2021

Summary: Assesses FHWA's investment in high friction surface treatment (HFST) research and technology deployment. The evaluation examines how federal research, technical assistance, and information-sharing activities influenced state transportation agencies' awareness, adoption, and use of HFST as a roadway safety intervention.

Findings: The evaluation found evidence that FHWA helped expand the research base, accelerate awareness, and encourage adoption of HFST technology. Many states experienced reductions in roadway-departure crashes following deployment. Although the study could not directly attribute those safety improvements to FHWA's activities, it identified federal research and knowledge-sharing as important contributors to broader implementation.

FHWA Research and Technology Evaluation: Precast Concrete Pavement

Bucci, Gregory; Keen, Matthew; Coppinger, Kaitlin; Condon, Joseph · FHWA-HRT-18-063 · October 2018

Summary: Evaluates FHWA's investments in precast concrete pavement technology, including research, demonstrations, and activities intended to encourage adoption. The study examines project-level benefits and costs, implementation experience, and the technology's potential to improve highway maintenance and reconstruction.

Findings: Precast concrete pavement was found to offer substantial benefits in locations where conventional construction would create prolonged closures or significant traffic disruption. Benefits can exceed costs considerably on high-volume roads and difficult-to-access sections. The evaluation also found that FHWA's research and demonstration efforts contributed to the technology's development and routine adoption in some states.

FHWA Research and Technology Evaluation TechBrief: High Friction Surface Treatments

Coppinger, Kaitlin; Keen, Matthew; Gissel, Jennifer; Rainville, Lydia · FHWA-HRT-21-098 · October 2021

Summary: Provides a concise overview of the FHWA evaluation of high friction surface treatments, summarizing federal research investments, technology adoption, and roadway safety impacts. The brief explains how HFST technology can improve pavement friction at locations with elevated crash risk, particularly curves and other roadway-departure locations.

Findings: The brief highlights HFST's potential to reduce targeted crash types and associated injuries and fatalities. It summarizes the broader evaluation of FHWA's role in expanding research, supporting technology transfer, and encouraging deployment. This is a companion publication to the full HFST evaluation rather than a separate research study.

Update on the Methodology for Amtrak Cost Accounting (APT): Annual Update for Appendices A and B, Fiscal Year 2019

Bucci, Gregory; Hyde, David; Keen, Matthew · DOT-VNTSC-FRA-20-05 · March 2020

Summary: Updates the documentation and financial summaries associated with Amtrak's Performance Tracking (APT) cost-accounting system for fiscal year 2019. The report presents allocated financial data within the APT framework, supporting analysis of Amtrak's operating expenses, revenues, and organizational cost structure.

Findings: The report provides an updated reference for understanding the distribution of costs and revenues across Amtrak's operations. Its figures represent a snapshot of unaudited APT data rather than independently audited financial results. The report also cautions that differences in accounting methods and organizational structures can limit direct comparisons with other Amtrak financial publications or previous years.

Update on the Methodology for Amtrak Cost Accounting: Amtrak Performance Tracking (APT), Vol. 2, Appendices A, B, and D

Bucci, Gregory; Hyde, David; Keen, Matthew · DOT-VNTSC-FRA-19-02 · March 2019

Summary: Updates supporting documentation for Amtrak's Performance Tracking cost-allocation methodology, presenting fiscal year 2018 financial summaries and related accounting information. The work builds on earlier documentation of the APT system, which allocates operating costs across Amtrak's business activities.

Findings: The updated appendices provide a structured view of allocated operating expenses and financial activity, supporting analysis of Amtrak's cost structure and performance. The report emphasizes that the underlying figures are unaudited and that changes in accounting classifications and reporting practices must be considered when making comparisons across fiscal years.

Update on the Methodology for Amtrak Cost Accounting (APT): Annual Update for Appendices A and B, Fiscal Year 2020

Bucci, Gregory; Hyde, David; Keen, Matthew · DOT-VNTSC-FRA-21-01 · January 2021

Summary: Presents the fiscal year 2020 update to Amtrak's Performance Tracking cost-accounting documentation, continuing a series of annual reports on allocated financial data. The report provides updated financial summaries organized according to APT's cost-allocation framework.

Findings: The report documents FY2020 financial information using the established APT methodology, providing a reference for examining Amtrak's allocated costs and revenues. As with earlier updates, the figures are unaudited and may differ from other published financial statements. The report highlights the need to account for changes in cost-center classifications and reporting structures when interpreting trends over time.

Evaluation Methods and Techniques: Advanced Transportation and Congestion Management Technologies Deployment Program

Petrella, Margaret; Chajka-Cadin, Lora; Rainville, Lydia; Pierce, S.; Keen, Matthew; Nodine, Emily; Noel, George; Zitzow-Childs, Stephen; Machek, Elizabeth; Eilbert, Andrew; Litvack-Winkler, Max; (Contributor: Harris, Dave) · FHWA-HOP-19-053 · December 2019

Summary: Develops a methodological framework for evaluating projects funded through FHWA's Advanced Transportation and Congestion Management Technologies Deployment (ATCMTD) Program. The report provides guidance on evaluation design, performance measurement, benefit–cost analysis, surveys, interviews, emissions measurement, and other techniques for assessing advanced transportation technology deployments.

Findings: The report establishes recommended evaluation approaches and performance measures to help grant recipients assess project benefits, costs, and effectiveness. It provides technology-specific guidance for adaptive signal control, connected and automated vehicles, and related applications. Rather than evaluating a completed deployment, the publication supplies a framework for conducting consistent, credible assessments of transportation technology projects.

Cost of Delay from HAZMAT Rail Incidents: Final Report

Taylor, Catherine; Baker, Gary; Eilbert, Andrew; Hyde, David; Keen, Matthew; Plotnick, Sarah; Roycroft, Claire; Wilke, Peter · DOT-VNTSC-PHMSA-23-01 · August 25, 2023

Summary: Develops an approach to estimating the economic costs of transportation delays resulting from hazardous-material incidents involving railroads. The research examines how track closures and service disruptions affect freight trains, passenger trains, and roadway users, including delays caused by waiting for infrastructure to reopen or rerouting around incident locations.

Findings: The study identifies several components of the social cost of rail disruptions, including additional crew labor, passenger time losses, the opportunity costs of locomotives and railcars, increased fuel consumption, and emissions. By considering effects beyond the immediate incident, the analysis provides a framework for incorporating broader transportation-system disruption costs into assessments of hazardous-material rail incidents.
